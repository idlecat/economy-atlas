# Ancient Crossroads (prototype)

An interactive map of civilisations from 3000 BC to AD 1900 and how they interacted, from the Silk Roads to the age of European empires: states and cultures, trade routes, journeys, battles and the spread of ideas, with a guided tour. It is separate from the Economy Atlas site and its pipeline.

- `data.js`: all content (civilisations with dated shapes, routes, events, tour stops, map views). Shapes are rough hand-drawn rings, clipped to land when drawn; years are negative for BC, and `to: Infinity` marks a state that still exists (shown as "from 1776"). An empire's overseas possessions are extra rings in the same shape; rings away from the capital get their own smaller label. The map's edge is at 160°W, so Pacific routes, journeys and shapes that cross it are split there (e.g. Alaska, the Manila galleons). Historical-border datasets such as historical-basemaps were not used because of their GPL licence.
- Today's countries take the Economy Atlas's entity ids and names (`config/entities.csv`, and `site/i18n/<lang>.json` for translations), so the build needs the rest of the repository.
- `cities.js`: major cities (`CITIES`) with population keyframes in thousands, interpolated linearly (a 0 keyframe marks destruction or abandonment), renamings (`later`, e.g. Tenochtitlan → Mexico City in 1521; translations list the later names in the same order), a note and a Wikipedia article. Figures are rough orders of magnitude after Chandler (1987) and Modelski (2003). Dots are sized by population; names are placed largest first where they don't collide with civilisation labels.
- `details.js`: what the panel shows for a selected civilisation besides `data.js`: its Wikipedia article (`WIKI`), dated key moments (`MOMENTS`), pictures for some of them (`MOMENT_WIKI`: an article whose lead image shows the moment, e.g. "Oracle bone", or `Article#File:Name.jpg` for another image in it; the card shows the latest moment's picture instead of the civilisation's own, and thumbnails in the list; no maps or modern statues) and population figures with their sources (`POP`), and successions of states over the same heartland (`SUCCESSIONS`, e.g. the Chinese dynasties), which the earlier/later buttons step across.
- `i18n/<lang>.js`: a translation (`I18N.<lang>`; so far `zh`, Simplified Chinese): interface strings (the keys of `UI_EN` in `app.js`), year formats, and the text of `data.js`, `cities.js` and `details.js` by id (key moments and population figures in the same order as the English). The language is chosen like on the main site (`?lang=`, then localStorage `lang`, which the site shares, then the browser) and from the menu in the header. Anything left untranslated stays in English and is listed in `window.__i18nMissing`, which the smoke test requires to be empty, so add every new civilisation, route, event, place label or key moment to each translation. To add a language, copy `zh.js` and translate it.
- `app.js`: the D3 map, timeline and panel. The panel holds the tour and what is on the map that year; the selected civilisation, city, route or event opens in a card over the map's top right (`#selcard`; Escape or clicking the empty map closes it). The timeline is piecewise linear (`TL_YEARS`, `TL_SHARE`), giving recent centuries more room. The URL hash holds what is on screen, so links can be shared (the header's Copy link button) and the browser's back and forward buttons work: `year` (negative for BC), `view` (a named view) or `at` (`lon,lat,zoom` after panning, zoom relative to the world view), `sel` (`civ.han`, `city.…`, `route.…`, `event.…`), `tour` (stop number), `hide` (layers turned off) and `show` (layers turned on that are off by default), e.g. `#year=-150&view=east&sel=civ.han&hide=cities&show=today`. Selecting, jumping, tour stops, dragging the timeline and playing add a history entry; playback, zooming and layer toggles update the current one.
- `page.html`: markup and styles, with placeholders for the inlined geography, data and app.
- `build.py`: downloads Natural Earth countries (merged into the land on the page, and drawn with their names by the off-by-default "Today's borders" layer), rivers and lakes, plus each civilisation's Wikipedia lead text, image and image credit (and the lead text of the same article on each translation's Wikipedia, found through its interlanguage links; `zh` asks for Simplified Chinese), into `.cache/` (delete `.cache/wiki/` to refresh), and writes the page (git-ignored). Images are shrunk to small JPEGs with Pillow; the page itself never contacts Wikipedia. Two forms:
  - default: `ancient-crossroads.html`, a self-contained page (images inlined) that loads D3, topojson and fonts from CDNs (used for the Claude artifact);
  - `--site`: `site/ancient/index.html` and `site/ancient/img/`, published with the site at `/ancient/`. Like the rest of the site it loads D3, topojson-client and its fonts from `site/vendor/` (fonts and `ancient-crossroads.css` come from `scripts/vendor.sh`), so it works where CDNs are blocked.

```sh
uv run --with pillow python prototypes/ancient-crossroads/build.py --site
python3 -m http.server -d site 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000/ancient/
```

The deploy workflow builds the site page, and the browser smoke test checks it when it has been built. The site footer links to it (`footer.ancient`). Borders, dates and figures were written from general knowledge and simplified; they have not been checked line by line against sources. The Chinese text was translated from the English the same way.

How this map was made

Every border, route and date here was drawn by hand as a simplified sketch, using standard historical atlases and reference works. It shows the general shape of things, not precise frontiers.

Reading the map

  • Coloured areas are states and empires. Paler areas with thin edges are cultures or groups of cities that were never one state. Colours group civilisations by region, so successors share a hue.
  • Ancient frontiers were zones, not lines, and many areas were claimed rather than ruled. Shapes change only at a few key dates.
  • Gold dashes are overland trade corridors and blue dots are sea lanes. They show general routes, not exact roads, and their thickness gives a rough sense of how busy they were.
  • Journeys follow a traveller's stops in order. Spreads join the earliest known evidence of an idea or technology at each place; the lines are not literal paths. Dates marked "c." are approximate, and many are debated.
